# Chapter 24: The Cliffs of Arn

*Not yet implemented.*

## Summary

The last approach: four miles of west coast from **Kodo Beach**, where the Sarz
meets the sea, north to the **Arn** river mouth. Sheer cliff on one side, the
western sea on the other, a path wide enough for two.

At the final headland the outer path stops, and **the Stone Gate** begins — a
gallery cut clean through the rock, filled and warded four centuries ago. The
party has to open it, from the outside, while the road they came in on falls
into the sea behind them.

## The Stone Gate

**The 347 cut it.** Three hundred and forty-seven knights and their train could
not walk a two-wide cliff path in a hurry, and they were in a hurry. The gallery
is the road they marched to Arn on. Amagi royal engineers cut it in eleven days,
and the mason's marks are still in the walls — Akira recognises them, which is
its own small horror: *our own people built this.*

**They filled it behind themselves.** Everyone has assumed for four centuries
that the fill was to stop anyone reaching the seal. Chapter 23 has just told the
player what the oath actually was: the 347 agreed to hold a door and expected to
be relieved.

> **The Stone Gate is the outer door.** It was not closed to keep people out. It
> was closed to keep something from walking back out, and it has been holding,
> quietly, the whole time, with nobody left alive who knew that was its job.

**This is why the chapter exists at all**, and why Takeshi did not have to do
it: he came alone, by sea, in under the arch at low tide. One man can. An army
cannot. The party is an army now, and the price of being one is that they have
to open the door.

**The ward is not a lock.** Mirelle can read it, and what it says is a request,
in the hand of somebody who was staying behind:

> *ひらくな。まだ、抑えている。*

She undoes it anyway. Shigeru does not let her do it alone — he puts his hand on
the stone with her, which is the whole of his character by chapter 24 and needs
no line on top of it.

## Setup

- **Map**: 32×24. A long, thin coast road running north, three or four ledges
  wide at its widest, ending at the headland and the gate. The sea is a hard
  boundary; the cliff is a hard boundary. **The most constrained map in the game.**
- **Objective**: **開通** — break the Stone Gate and get everyone through
- **Deploy**: 16 slots, `shigeru` forced
- **Enemies**: 30, triggered — 5 per trigger, 4 triggers
- **Boss**: the rearguard commander on the headland, who has been told to make
  them late and knows exactly why
- **Par**: 22 turns

## The gate, as combat

Two phases at the gate tile, and neither is a cutscene.

**Phase 1 — 解呪.** The stone will not break while the ward holds. A light-magic
or staff unit standing **on the gate tile for three consecutive turns** takes it
off. Knock them off the tile, or make them act, and the count resets to zero.
This is Mirelle's job, or the Suza keeper's from Ch14 — and it is the one job in
the game that only the units the player has been treating as support can do.

**Phase 2 — 掘る.** Once the ward is off, any unit on the gate tile or the two
tiles flanking it contributes its **力** to a 破石値 at the end of the player
phase. The gate opens at a fixed total. Three strong units for three turns, or
the whole front line for two — and high-Con axe users are the best at it, which
makes Gareth the structural answer to something for the first time in the
campaign.

The point of the split is that **the chapter cannot be solved by the units the
player has been winning with.** It needs a healer to stand still in the open for
three turns, and it needs the bruisers off the line and swinging at a wall.

## The pressure is the ground, not a number

There is no turn counter on the HUD. **The road closes behind them.**

| | |
|---|---|
| From turn 3, every second turn | the two southernmost columns of the road go **灰** — HP bleeding away each turn, defence and avoid down |
| Two turns after that | the same columns become **深淵の裂け目**. Impassable. Anything still standing there is gone |

The safe ground shrinks toward the gate at a fixed, readable rate, with a
two-turn warning band that is always visible. There is no retreat and the player
can see exactly how much floor is left. A unit lost here is lost to *standing
still*, which is the correct way for this chapter to punish.

Enemies come **from the gate side only**. The rearguard is already on the
headland, between the party and the door, and Colossi come up out of the rifts
in the path. **Nothing comes from behind**, because the thing behind them is the
ground.

## Beats

**Prologue.** Lisette walks the outer path with a rule and gives it a number of
turns before the sea has it. She is right, and nobody enjoys that any more.

**The gate.** Akira reads the mason's marks. Royal engineers, Amagi, and a date.
*"Our own people cut this. And then our own people filled it in."*

**The ward.** Mirelle reads it out loud because somebody has to hear it, and the
company understands at the same moment the player does: this is not a lock, and
the man who set it was staying behind. Lisette is the one who says the thing
nobody wants said — that if it is still holding, then opening it is not free.

**Gareth.** Twenty-four chapters of asking the obvious question, and here he is
the one who can actually break rock. He says less in this chapter than in any
other, and that should be conspicuous.

**Boss.** The rearguard commander is not a fanatic either. *"He does not need you
dead. He needs you tired. There is a difference and you will feel it tomorrow."*

**The far end.** The gallery is full of four-hundred-year-old marching order —
kit laid down against the wall in ranks, neatly, by people who expected to come
back through it. Nobody says anything. Then the party walks out the north mouth
and sees Arn: the sea arch, the tide going through it, the shrine on the cliff
above, and the unlit brazier on its floor.

Relighting the seal stops being an idea and becomes a thing the player can point
at — a cold brazier, and a sword with 347 embers in it.

## What the engine still needs

None of this exists yet, and all of it is chapter-24-shaped rather than general:

- **A `breach` objective kind.** `ChapterDef.objective` is `seize | rout`.
- **A multi-turn action on a tile** that a unit holds and can be interrupted —
  the ward. Nothing in the game currently spans turns like this.
- **An accumulating contribution from adjacency** — the 破石値.
- **Terrain that rewrites itself on a schedule.** Ch5 needs this first; here it
  is the whole chapter, and it needs the two-stage 灰 → 裂け目 warning to be
  legible on the map without a HUD counter.
- **Blighted terrain** as a per-turn hazard (Ch11 needs this first).
