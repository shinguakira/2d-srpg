# Chapter 19: The Flame on Shirato

*Not yet implemented.*

## Summary

The twin-peaked holy mountain above Asha Bay, the northern Sacred Flame still
burning, and the last keeper alive to tend it. Both armies arrive at once.

**This is the mountain the player has been looking at since Arc 1.** Shirato's
two pale crags stand out of dark forest and are visible from half the maps in
the game — from Kandel in Ch5, from the bay in Ch6 and Ch18, from the coast road.
By the time the party climbs it they have spent fourteen chapters checking it on
the horizon to find out how badly things are going. Ch19 is the payoff for that,
and it should open with the mountain filling the screen.

## Intent

- **Objective**: protect the shrine keeper (`shiratoKeeperSaved` — an ending flag)
- **Terrain**: the primeval forest on the mountain's shoulders — heavy cover,
  brutal movement cost, and a summit approach only one unit wide. Above the
  treeline it inverts: bare pale rock, no cover at all, and both armies fully
  exposed on the last two hundred metres.
- **Lore payoff**: the keeper explains what the 347 actually did, and what
  relighting a shrine costs. The Flamebrand holds 347 embers; relighting Arn
  spends every one of them and the sword goes dark for good.
- **Structural point**: Shigeru learns the price of the best ending *here*, with
  six chapters left to carry it, rather than being handed it as a twist at the end.
- **Mirelle's homecoming**: this is her shrine. She has not been back since Ch1.

## The white and the grey

The mountain's own rock is pale, and at a distance it looks exactly like blighted
ground.

**Set this up in Arc 1 and pay it here.** Somewhere before Ch10, somebody sees
Shirato on the skyline and goes quiet — and it is only the stone. Nobody makes a
speech about it; the scene simply moves on, and the party has now taught the
player to look at that mountain.

Then in Ch19 the approach march does it for real: the summit is white, and this
time nobody in the company will say out loud which they think it is. The answer
comes when they get above the treeline and find the flame still lit.

By Ch24 the same trick has a third state — the party can no longer see Shirato
from the west coast at all, and that absence is what the cliff road feels like.

## Two floras on one slope

Plants from across the western water grow on this mountain beside the ones from
home, on the same slope, and have for longer than anyone has counted. **The
keeper points at it while explaining something else** — never as a lecture.

It is the quietest line the setting has for the thing the whole campaign is
about: the crossing has always been happening, the Blackflame was not the first
thing to come over from that shore, and a seal is a decision to hold a border
that the land itself has never respected.

It should also make Takeshi harder to dismiss, one arc before Ch23 makes him
harder still.

## Beats

**The approach.** Mirelle stops talking a kilometre out. Nobody makes her
explain.

**Above the treeline.** Cover ends. Both armies are on bare rock in the open, and
the chapter's tactical problem stops being movement cost and becomes exposure.

**The keeper.** Old, alone, and has kept the flame burning through an occupation
by declining to notice it. She tells them the cost of relighting Arn plainly,
because she has been waiting a long time for somebody to come and ask.

**The price, stated early.** 347 embers, all of them, and the sword goes dark.
Shigeru does not decide anything here. He carries it for six chapters.

**Mirelle.** The shrine sent her out in Ch1 to find the Flamebrand's bearer. She
found him. She comes back with him, and with what it is going to cost him, and
the homecoming is worse for having succeeded.
