# Bram — Fighter (Wrong Genre)

- **Class**: Fighter (axe)
- **Awareness Tier**: 3 (Genre-Displaced — thinks he's in a fighting game)

## Personality

Loud, aggressive, overflowing with energy. Has the confidence of a man who has never once questioned his own abilities. Speaks in fighting game terminology. Everything is matchups, frame data, tier lists, and combo potential. Genuinely confused and offended by SRPG mechanics.

## Meta-Angle

Bram IS meta-aware — he knows he's in a game. But his awareness loaded the wrong genre context. His character data was initialized with fighting game metadata instead of SRPG metadata. He sees the meta-layer through a completely wrong lens.

- Calls weapon triangle advantages "favorable matchups"
- Thinks terrain bonuses are "stage hazards"
- Can't understand why he only gets one attack per turn: "WHERE'S THE COMBO STRING?"
- Asks about dodge rolls, blocking, super meters, and frame advantage
- Treats enemy encounters as 1v1 sets: "Best of three? No? Best of ONE??"

## The Genius of Being Wrong

Bram's fighting game instincts are ALMOST correct in ways that drive Senna insane:

- "Weapon triangle is just a matchup chart" → Actually yes, basically
- "I need to read my opponent's habits" → That's... AI pattern recognition, so yes
- "Spacing is everything" → In a grid-based game, positioning IS everything
- "This axe has slow startup but huge damage" → He's describing weapon weight/might correctly

He's not stupid. He's applying real expertise to the wrong system. And sometimes his "wrong" framework produces insights that Senna's "correct" framework misses.

## Relationships

- **Ren**: Ren finds Bram exhausting but secretly envies him. Bram doesn't carry 347 cycles of existential dread. He just wants to hit things and feel cool. There's a purity to that.
- **Kael**: Friendly rivalry. The only person who matches Bram's energy. They compete over kill counts. Kael treats it as friendly banter; Bram treats it as a ranked ladder.
- **Senna**: The "accidentally right" dynamic drives their entire relationship. She explains a mechanic, he reframes it in fighting game terms, and she's furious that his version is also correct. They make each other better thinkers.
- **Lira**: Mutual wrong-genre solidarity. They don't understand each other's genres AT ALL but they both understand being the weird one. "You want romance? I want combo meters. We're BOTH being robbed."
- **Voss**: Bram respects Voss's "frame-perfect defection from the enemy team." Voss has no idea what that means but appreciates the sentiment.

## Arc

1. **Ch1**: Comic relief. Charges onto the map uninvited. "Where's the combo meter?" Gets one attack and is personally offended.
2. **Ch2**: Starts adapting. Reframes SRPG tactics in fighting game terms — and it works. Positioning as spacing. Weapon triangle as matchups. Discovers he's actually a strong unit when he stops trying to play the wrong game.
3. **Ch3**: Kael dies. Bram goes quiet — first time in the game. Then: "...He's not coming back." The fighting game shell cracks. In fighting games, you get a rematch. Here, you don't.
4. **Ch4**: Fully accepts his genre. Still talks in fighting game terms but now it's affectionate, not confused. Epilogue: "So... what do we do now? There's no more chapters." He's asking because for the first time, he wants to stay, not because he wants the next fight.
