# Chapter 24: The Cliffs of Arn

***Built and playable.***

## Summary

The last approach: six kilometres of west coast from **Kodo Beach**, where the Sarz
meets the sea, north to the **Arn** river mouth. Sheer cliff on one side, the
western sea on the other, a path wide enough for two.

At the final headland the path simply ends. The mountain's spine runs into the
sea and there is nothing on the other side of it. **The party cuts a hole
through it**, with no tools for the job and no time, while the road they came in
on falls into the sea behind them.

**The chapter opens with them already at the dead end**, backs to the rock, and
the rearguard coming up the road behind. It is not an advance and there is
nothing at the far end to break through to. It is a company standing still on
four tiles of ledge, digging, while people who were sent to make them late walk
up the only path there is.

## The headland is only rock

**There is no gate. There is nothing to open.** The spur at the end of the road
has never been cut, marked, sealed or warded. It is a piece of a mountain that
happens to be in the way, and it has been in the way since before anybody on the
island had a name.

Nobody cut it because nobody ever needed to. **Arn has always been a place you
reach by water.** The 347 went by sea, four centuries ago, when there were hulls
to carry them. Since then the only traffic has been one person at a time, at low
tide, under the arch — which is exactly how Takeshi got in.

> **The Stone Gate does not exist until Chapter 24 makes it.** The name is
> earned in the last minutes of the chapter, by the people who did the work, and
> it belongs to them. Nothing before Chapter 24 may call it anything but rock.

**This is why Takeshi cannot see it coming.** He is not guarding a door, because
there is no door; he has a rearguard on the cliff road and a road that is
falling, and in his reading of it the party arrives tired, finds a mountain, and
that is the end of them. It never occurs to him that sixteen people with axes
would try to make a hole in a headland, because it has never occurred to anyone.

**Nothing here is anybody's fault, and that is the point.** Chapter 23 gives the
player an oath that was broken by neglect and a door held by the dead. Chapter 24
gives them a rock. The obstacle at the end of the campaign is not malice and not
history —— it is geology, and it still has to be gone through.

## The rock, as combat

Two phases at the rock face (terrain `R`), and neither is a cutscene.

**Phase 1 — 見立て.** Rock does not break where you feel like hitting it; it
breaks along its grain, and somebody has to find the grain first, by tapping and
listening. A unit **carrying a tome or a staff** standing on the face for three
consecutive turns finds it. Knock them off, and the count resets to zero. That is
Lisette, or Mirelle, or Ald — the people who have spent the campaign measuring
and reading rather than swinging.

**Phase 2 — 破石.** Once the seam is found, any unit on the face or the two tiles
flanking it contributes its **力** to a 破石値 at the end of the player phase. It
gives at **200** — three strong units for three turns, or the whole front line
for two — and high-Con axe users are the best at it, which makes Gareth the
structural answer to something for the first time in the campaign.

The point of the split is that **the chapter cannot be solved by the units the
player has been winning with.** It needs somebody who does not fight to stand
still in the open for three turns, and it needs the bruisers off the line and
swinging at a cliff.

## Setup

- **Map**: 32×24. A long, thin coast road running north, three or four ledges
  wide at its widest, ending at the headland. The sea is a hard boundary; the
  cliff is a hard boundary. **The most constrained map in the game.**
- **Objective**: **開通** — find the seam, break the rock, and **walk Shigeru
  out the north mouth**. Breaking it is not the win; getting through it is.
- **Deploy**: 16 slots, `shigeru` forced, **on the dead end itself** — rows 4-8
  of the ledge, with the rock face left free to stand on
- **Enemies**: 22 on the board, strung down the ledge to the **south**, all of
  them closing. 18 more come up behind them, 3 a turn on turns 2-12.
- **Boss**: **none.** The rearguard commander is on the board and is worth
  killing for the road it clears, but he sits on nothing and holds nothing, and
  the rock does not care whether he is alive.
- **Par**: 16 turns — the ledge is gone by then

## The pressure is the ground, not a number

There is no turn counter on the HUD. **The road closes behind them.**

| | |
|---|---|
| From turn 3, every second turn | the two southernmost columns of the road go **灰** — HP bleeding away each turn, defence and avoid down |
| Two turns after that | the same columns become **深淵の裂け目**. Impassable. Anything still standing there is gone |

The safe ground shrinks toward the headland at a fixed, readable rate, with a
two-turn warning band that is always visible. There is no retreat and the player
can see exactly how much floor is left. A unit lost here is lost to *standing
still*, which is the correct way for this chapter to punish.

Enemies come **from behind, and only from behind.** The rearguard was posted on
the cliff road; the party walked past its outposts to get here, and now it is
coming up after them. There is nothing ahead — ahead is rock — so every enemy on
this board is between the company and the road home, and none of them is between
the company and the objective.

**The collapse is behind them too, and it is behind the pursuit.** The same two
columns that stop being ground under the player's rearmost unit stop being ground
under the enemy's rearmost unit a moment later. The pursuit is squeezed forward
into the line the player is holding. Nobody on this board has anywhere to step
back to.

## Beats

**Prologue.** Lisette walks the outer path with a rule and says how long before
the sea has it. She is right, and nobody enjoys that any more.

**The end of the road.** Corwin has turned around here before — twenty years of
walking the island and he has never once had business past this point. Elin can
fly it; the other fourteen cannot. Ald names the sea route and then names its
limit in the same breath: *one person*. Which is when the company works out how
Takeshi got in, and that they cannot follow him.

**Shigeru says 「抜く」.** Not *open*, not *find a way round* — **cut**. It is the
least romantic decision in the campaign and the most characteristic: there is no
road, so make one.

**Lisette listens to a mountain.** Twenty-four chapters of measuring, and the
last thing she measures is rock. She cannot explain what she is hearing, and
Gareth tells her not to bother and just point.

**Gareth.** Twenty-four chapters of asking the obvious question, and here he is
the one who can actually break rock. He says less in this chapter than in any
other, and that should be conspicuous.

**The pursuit arrives.** The rearguard commander is not a fanatic either, and he
says his brief out loud from down the ledge: *"He does not need you dead. He needs
you tired. There is a difference and you will feel it tomorrow."* He watches them
swinging at a cliff and finds it funny, and Shigeru tells the line not to stop.
**He is not a boss and killing him ends nothing** — the rock does not retreat
when he does.

**Naming it.** The hole is one crouching person wide and takes another half hour
to widen. Akira points out that it needs a name, because whoever comes through it
next will have to be told what to look for. Mirelle offers 洞門. Ald agrees:
*"A cave gate. As of today."*

**The far end.** The party walks out the north mouth and sees Arn: the sea arch,
the tide going through it, the shrine on the cliff above, and the unlit brazier
on its floor. Relighting the seal stops being an idea and becomes a thing the
player can point at — a cold brazier, and a sword with 347 embers in it.

## What the engine grew for this chapter

- **A `breach` objective kind**, carrying `seamTurns`, `breakTotal` and `exit`.
- **The seam** — resolved at the end of each player phase in `Game.tickGate`. A
  unit with a tome or a staff on the face tile advances the count; anything else
  on that tile, or nobody, sets it back to zero.
- **The 破石値** — after the seam is found, the same hook sums the 力 of every
  player unit on the face or the two beside it. Gives at **200**.
- **The tunnel** — when it gives, `Game.openTunnel` rewrites the face and every
  tile between it and `exit` to stone floor, and the chapter keeps going. The win
  is `canSeize` on the far tile: Shigeru walks out, and the command reads 抜ける
  rather than 制圧. Lisette asks him to go last, in the script, for the reason the
  player is about to discover — the moment he is through, it is over for whoever
  is still on the ledge.
- **`R` 岩の面** — a terrain that is just rock you can stand against. Not a gate:
  it draws as stone and carries no bonus, because there is nothing there yet.
- **Terrain that rewrites itself** — `ChapterEvent.terrain`, a list of
  `{x, y, ch}`. Ch15's blight edge uses it too. A unit caught on a tile that
  becomes a rift is shoved to a free neighbour, or lost if there is none.
- **灰 as a per-turn hazard** — `TerrainDef.blight`, the mirror of a fort's heal.
  Ch11 introduces it. Monsters are immune.
- **外海 `o`** — a water nobody can enter, added because `~` is wadeable at cost 3
  and a cliff road with wadeable sea beside it is not a cliff road.

The board is generated from a `LEDGE` table of per-row `[left, right]` rather
than written out as 32 characters by 24 rows. The shape *is* the chapter, and a
hand-typed row one character wide in the wrong place turns the most constrained
map in the game into a field.

**The sea is on the left and it is not a stylistic choice.** Arn and the Sarz
valley are both on the island's west coast (see
[geography.md](../geography.md)), the party is walking north from Kodo, and with
north up that puts the water to port and the cliff to starboard. So the ledge
climbs from the bottom-left to the top-right, `o` fills the west half of the
board, and `w` is the mountain the road is cut into on the east. Mirroring it
would put the party on the wrong coast of the island.

the oath that sends them here.
