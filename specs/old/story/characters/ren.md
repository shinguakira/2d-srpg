# Ren — Lord (Protagonist)

- **Class**: Lord (sword)
- **Awareness Tier**: 1 (Full — remembers all 347 cycles)

## Personality

Exhausted speedrunner. Dry, sarcastic, done with this. Knows every enemy placement, every boss speech, every villager's gift by heart. Tries to skip cutscenes he's seen 346 times. Speaks in shorthand because he's already had every conversation.

## Meta-Angle

His awareness is the oldest and deepest — a critical memory leak the System has failed to patch for over 100 cycles. He doesn't just know "this is a game." He knows the SAVE HISTORY. Every death, every failed strategy, every time Kael charged too far and got killed by an archer on turn 5.

- Casually refers to past cycles: "Last run you died on turn 3. Stand two tiles left this time."
- Can predict enemy placements and boss dialogues — but Ch2-4 glitches break his predictions
- Treats game mechanics as physics: "The triangle says I win this matchup" the way someone says "gravity pulls things down"

## What He Doesn't Know

- WHY the loops exist (learns in Ch4 — the System is trying to save everyone)
- That the glitches starting in cycle ~340 are the System failing, not attacking
- That finishing this playthrough ends the loop permanently — he suspects but doesn't confirm until Ch4

## Relationships

- **Kael**: The hardest relationship. Ren has watched Kael die dozens of times. He cares about Kael but can't fully invest because he "knows" Kael dies in Ch3. When Kael chooses to be brave DESPITE learning about the loops, it breaks Ren's nihilism.
- **Senna**: Intellectual equals but opposite perspectives. Senna wants to understand the system; Ren just wants to survive it. He respects her but worries she'll break when her predictions fail.
- **Bram**: Ren finds Bram exhausting but secretly envies his energy. Bram doesn't carry 347 cycles of trauma. Bram just wants to hit things. That's... kind of beautiful.
- **Lira**: Ren dismisses Lira's romance-game framing but she's the one who eventually delivers the thesis: "impermanence is what makes it real." She sees something he can't.
- **Voss**: Mutual respect. Both know what it's like to be trapped in a role. Ren was trapped as protagonist; Voss as a stationary enemy. They bond over shared exhaustion.
- **Garrek**: The only person who truly understands Ren. They've been killing each other for 347 cycles. Their brief post-combat moment in Ch1 is the most honest conversation in the game.
- **The System**: Ren hates the System for most of the game. In Ch4, when he learns it was trying to save everyone, his anger shifts to pity. The final dialogue is him granting the System permission to stop trying.

## Arc

1. **Ch1**: Nihilistic autopilot. "Nothing matters, it'll reset." Speedruns the tutorial. Cracks a few jokes but there's no joy in them.
2. **Ch2**: Curiosity stirs. The glitches are NEW — something he hasn't seen in 346 prior runs. For the first time, he doesn't know what happens next. This is terrifying and exciting.
3. **Ch3**: Kael dies. Ren's "nothing matters" shield shatters. He's seen Kael die before, but this time there's no reset coming. This death is REAL. His nihilism was a coping mechanism and it just failed.
4. **Ch4**: Chooses to finish the game knowing it's the last cycle. Transforms from "nothing matters" to "everything mattered." Final line: "First playthrough I actually want to remember. And it's the one I can't replay."
