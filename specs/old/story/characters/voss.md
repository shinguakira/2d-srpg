# Voss — Soldier (The Defector)

- **Class**: Soldier (lance)
- **Awareness Tier**: 4 → 1 (Unaware → Full — gains awareness through defection)
- **Joins**: Mid Ch2

## Personality

Deadpan. Pragmatic. Quietly furious. Speaks in short, flat sentences. Carries the specific resentment of someone who has been standing on the same tile for 300 cycles watching his allies die while his AI behavior said "stationary."

He's not loud about his anger. He's the kind of angry that shows up as perfect posture and clipped words.

## Meta-Angle

Voss's awareness is UNIQUE — he gained it through the act of defection itself.

As an enemy unit, his data was managed by the enemy AI system. When he chose to switch sides (walking toward the player units instead of attacking), he broke his AI behavior flag. That code violation created a data exception that cascaded into partial memory recovery. Defecting didn't just change his faction — it FREED his data from the enemy management system, exposing him to the raw system layer.

- Understands enemy AI from the inside: "Boss AI stays on the throne. Always. He CAN'T leave even if he wanted to."
- Knows which enemies are stationary, aggressive, guard-radius — because he was one of them
- Bitter about his assigned AI: "Stationary. Fifteen turns. Standing. Watching."
- His defection in prior cycles was different each time (cycle 298: broke because of AI frustration. This cycle: broke because of growth rates)
- Can explain how enemy units EXPERIENCE being controlled by AI — it's not that they follow orders, it's that they literally cannot conceive of alternatives until something breaks

## What He Provides

Voss is the party's expert on enemy behavior. He fills a narrative role that no other character can:

- Explains boss patterns before engagement: "Morryn is boss-AI. He won't chase you. Use that."
- Identifies which enemies can be reasoned with (partially aware) vs which are fully scripted (unaware)
- In Ch3-4, recognizes when enemy spawns are "wrong" — corrupted by the System: "That soldier has a Brave Sword. Soldiers don't GET Brave Swords. Something spawned him incorrectly."

## Relationships

- **Ren**: Mutual respect rooted in shared exhaustion. Both know what it's like to be trapped in a role — Ren as the protagonist who can't stop replaying, Voss as the enemy who couldn't stop standing still. They don't talk much but they understand each other.
- **Kael**: Initially suspicious (former enemy). Kael gives Voss a chance because Kael gives EVERYONE a chance. Voss respects this but doesn't understand it: "Why trust me? My stats are enemy-tier." / "Because you chose to be here."
- **Senna**: The best analytical partnership in the party. Senna provides system theory; Voss provides lived data. Together they map out enemy AI behaviors completely. Their conversations are dry, technical, and deeply nerdy.
- **Bram**: Bram respects Voss's "frame-perfect defection from the enemy team mid-match." Voss has absolutely no idea what any of those words mean but appreciates that someone thinks it was cool.
- **Lira**: Lira immediately assigns Voss the "redemption route" and asks about his tragic backstory. Voss doesn't have one — "I stood on a tile for 300 cycles. That's not a backstory. That's a prison sentence." Lira: "That IS a tragic backstory!!"

## Arc

1. **Pre-defection** (Ch1-2 early): Exists as an enemy unit. The player fights past him. He's just another soldier — unless you notice he doesn't attack.
2. **Ch2 defection**: Walks toward player units. "I'm done." Checks his own stats aloud. "40% STR growth on the enemy side. I have DIGNITY." Joins the party. His defection triggers his awareness cascade.
3. **Ch3**: Provides crucial intel on Morryn's AI pattern. Recognizes corrupted enemy spawns. Struggles with the question: "Did I defect because I chose to, or because the data corruption made me?" Ren: "Does it matter?" Voss: "...It matters to me."
4. **Ch4**: First time choosing where to stand. After 300 cycles of stationary AI, the epilogue gives him the simplest, most profound moment: "I'm going to stand on a different tile. Any tile. Because I CAN." Freedom defined as the ability to move one square.
