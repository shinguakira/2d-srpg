# Geography — what Amagi is borrowed from

**Amagi is Tsushima with the names taken off.**

[world.md](world.md) says the island is invented and the shape is not, and then
declines to say whose shape. This file says it, once, so that nobody has to
reverse-engineer it from a list of altitudes again — and so that the next place
invented for a chapter is invented the same way as the last one.

**The names must stay invented.** A map labelled with real towns reads as a
documentary instead of a game, which is the wrong feeling entirely, and the
dressing is Fire Emblem — knights, lances, pegasus riders — not Kamakura. What
is borrowed is the **topography**: what is next to what, how far, how steep,
where you can land a boat and where you cannot.

## What the real island already does for us

**The opening is history with the names changed.** A fleet crosses from the west,
lands on the one flat sand on that coast, and the local lord rides out with what
he has and dies on the beach. That is Komoda in November 1274 and Sō Sukekuni's
eighty riders. Ch1's prologue is that event; **Ch10 returns to the same beach one
arc later**, which is a structure the real geography hands over for free, because
there is nowhere else on that coast to land.

**A fortress that watches an empty sea out of habit.** Kaneda-jō was raised in 667
after Hakusukinoe against an attack from the west that never came, and was
garrisoned, rebuilt and re-garrisoned for the next twelve hundred years. General
Roderic in Ch5 — nine men lost to the rift in his own courtyard, three reports
filed, ordered to hold the fortress — is that institution given a face.

Amagi puts Kandel in the same years as the sealing, which the real fort does not
supply and which is worth the small departure: **Kandel exists because of the
Blackflame.** It was built to watch for the next one. So Ch5 is a man who has
been ordered not to look west, holding a fortress that was raised for no other
purpose than looking west, while the thing it was built for opens a hole in his
courtyard. Nobody in the chapter says this. The player can have it.

**Ground nobody is allowed to walk on, that is therefore still standing.** The
Dreadful Place on Tatera-san was genuinely closed to laymen, and four centuries
of forest survived precisely because of it. Ch20-23 do not have to invent the
prohibition or the forest; they build inside one that exists.

**Cutting through the land when it is in the way is what this island does.** At
Kofunakoshi, boats were hauled overland across the neck between the two seas.
In 1672 the Sō cut Ōfunakoshi-seto so larger hulls could pass. In 1900 the navy
cut Manzeki-seto. **The Stone Gate in Ch24 is the fourth time**, four centuries
earlier, and it is in character for the place.

## Where the design leaves the real island

Knowing which of these are deliberate matters when the next chapter needs a
location.

- **Yatan sits in the wrong country.** The real Yatate-yama is on the *upper*
  island and Tatera-san is at the far southern end — forty kilometres apart, not
  ringing a shared basin. The design pulls them together so Ch8 and Ch20-23 share
  one inland region. **Deliberate, and worth keeping.**
- **The Cut is natural here and artificial there.** Manzeki-seto was dug in 1900.
  Making Amagi's channel man-made as well is an open question rather than an
  error: it would let the setting say *this island cuts through what is in its
  way* in two places instead of one, and put the Stone Gate's engineers in the
  same lineage as whoever split the country. Not adopted yet.
- **Arn's sea arch is invented.** There are sea caves and stacks along that coast;
  there is no famous arch at Aren. The seal needs somewhere to be, and this is
  where the design stops borrowing.
- **Arc 4 walks a long way.** Ilza (east) → Sarz (west) → Kesh (north) → Shirato
  (north) → Taragi (south) is roughly two hundred kilometres on a seventy
  kilometre island. Marching-wise that is nothing; the risk is the *feeling* of
  doubling back, and Ch17 is written knowing it — the bridge is still there,
  Halvar is not.

## Why Arn takes twenty chapters

The company turns west at the end of Ch5 and arrives at Ch24. The gap between
those two numbers is the campaign, and **it has to be the island's fault rather
than the plot's.** Two failed approaches do that work, and both of them are the
real coast rather than an invention.

### From the north — Imasa, Ch6

Imasa sits on Asha Bay's south shore, under Shirato. Arn is fifteen kilometres
south-west of it. On a map that is an afternoon.

**There is no road, and there never was one.** The west coast between the bay and
Arn is a hundred metres of cliff with the mountains coming down onto it, and
nobody has ever had a reason to cut a path along a shore with nowhere to land.
The two places you *can* land on that coast — Kodo and Arn — are both reached
from inland, which is exactly why the 347 had to cut the Stone Gate.

So the party turns west at Kandel, walks to Imasa, and finds out that **west is
not a direction you can travel on this island.** To reach a thing fifteen kilometres
away they have to go back east through Kesh, south the length of the country,
and come at the coast down the Sarz. *That* is the route Arcs 3 and 4 walk, and
Lisette should be the one who measures it and says the number out loud.

### From the south — Kodo, Ch10

The second attempt has a road: six kilometres of cliff path north from Kodo Beach.
They take it, and it ends at the **Stone Gate**, filled and warded, with Arn
visible past the headland and no way through.

**This is what makes Takeshi's offer at Ch10 land.** He walks down the hill road
at the one place they have just been stopped, and offers to carry the sword the
six kilometres they cannot walk it. He came in by sea, alone, under the arch at low
tide — and he knows perfectly well that an army cannot.

### The rule the route is built on

> **Arn cannot be reached until the Stone Gate is opened, and the Stone Gate
> cannot be opened by the company that is standing at Kodo in Ch10.**

Opening it needs the Suza warden who can read the ward (recruited Ch14), enough bodies
to hold a closing road (deployment reaches 16 by Ch23), and Lisette's rules about
running water and hallowed ground (Ch17, Ch21). Everything Arcs 3 and 4 hand the
player is a component of Ch24, and the player should be able to feel that
without being told.

## Naming a new place

When a chapter needs somewhere that is not on the list:

1. Find the real place with the topography the chapter needs. The island is small
   and well surveyed; the answer usually exists.
2. **Shift the syllables until it is not the real name** — Izuhara to Ilza, Sasu
   to Sarz, Komoda to Kodo, Kaneda to Kandel. Close enough that the shape survives,
   far enough that nobody is looking up a real town.
3. **Keep the numbers.** Altitudes and distances came over intact for Shirato,
   Yatan and Taragi, and that is why the island reads as a real place rather than
   a backdrop.
4. Check it against the terrain rules in [world.md](world.md): forest nearly
   everywhere, steep ground with almost no plain, cliffs on the west, and only two
   ways in from that sea.

## Shirato — the one that carries more than a row in a table

`white peak`. Two bare crags of pale quartz-porphyry standing out of dark
primeval forest, 519m, and **visible from most of the Lower Country and from
across Asha Bay**. On the real island it is the thing you look at: a national
natural monument since 1923, a shrine below the summit, an approach that narrows
to one file, and a slope where continental plants and Japanese ones grow
together — species from the far shore that occur nowhere else in the country.

Three of those are worth more to this campaign than they are being used for.

**The summit is white, and the blight is grey.** From a distance nobody can tell
which. This should be a running visual from Ch5 onward, and the party should get
it wrong at least once — someone sees the peak on the horizon and goes quiet, and
then it is only the rock. By Ch19 the player has learned to look at that mountain
to find out how the campaign is going, which is the cheapest possible campaign
clock: **a thing on the skyline you check.**

**It is the flame you can see from anywhere.** Three shrines hold three flames.
Arn is broken before Ch1 and Suza goes out in Ch15 — and Shirato is the one that
is *visible*, from the bay, from Kandel, from half the maps in the game. When the
southern flame dies the party does not need to be told there are two left. They
can turn around and look at the one that is still lit.

**Two floras on one slope.** Plants from across the western water grow on that
mountain beside the ones from home, and have for longer than anyone has been
counting. It is the quietest way the setting can say that **the crossing has
always been happening**, and it belongs in the shrine keeper's mouth in Ch19,
next to what the 347 actually did — not as a lecture, as the thing she points at
while explaining something else.
