# World — "The Sacred Flame"

A straight, classical tactical-RPG setting in the vein of *Fire Emblem: The Sacred
Stones*. Real kingdoms, real armies, a sealed evil, and a war started by a man who
believed he was ending war forever. Nothing in this world is aware it is a game, and
nothing in the writing should suggest otherwise.

## Setting note — the island is invented, the shape is not

**Amagi** is not a real place and no name in this document is. Every place name
here is made up, because a map labelled with real towns reads as a documentary
rather than a game, and that is the wrong feeling entirely.

The *shape* is borrowed and worth keeping, because it is already the map a
tactician would want: one long landmass cut in two by a channel, an enormous
drowned bay in the middle, an interior that is almost entirely forest, a spine of
500-650m peaks, and a west coast of sheer cliffs staring at a foreign shore fifty
miles away.

**The dressing is Fire Emblem.** Knights, lances, cavalry, pegasus riders,
castles, shrines, an empire across the water. No samurai, no real-world religion,
no continental politics. If a scene would only work in a historical drama, it is
wrong for this game.

## The Premise

Four centuries ago the **Blackflame** came ashore out of the western sea. It does
not burn what it touches — it hollows it. Ground goes grey and stops growing. People
go grey and stand back up as **revenants**, wearing their own faces.

It was stopped at the cliffs of **Arn** on the west coast by **three hundred and
forty-seven knights** of Amagi, who burned their own lives to drive it back into the
rock and seal it there. What was left of them — the embers — was bound into a single
sword, the **Flamebrand**, carried by the kings of Amagi ever since. The knights
themselves were laid inland, under **Taragi**, in ground no layman may walk on.

For four centuries the seal held.

## The Inciting Event

Eleven months before the campaign opens, **Takeshi**, Emperor of **Kurogane**, came
ashore at Arn alone, broke the ward, and took the Blackflame into his own body.
Three days later he gave the order to march.

The Kurogane fleet landed at **Kodo Beach** on the west coast. **King Sadao** of
Amagi rode out from Ilza to meet them on the sand and died there. The royal seat
at **Ilza** fell the same night, and the Flamebrand passed to his son.

## The Realm of Amagi

Amagi is the island. One landmass, seventy miles north to south, cut across its
waist by a narrow channel — **the Cut** — into two countries:

- **The Lower Country** — everything south of the Cut. The royal
  seat, the mountains, the shrines, the seal. **The entire campaign happens here.**
- **The Upper Country** — north of the Cut. Watchtowers, cold coasts,
  and Mitan standing over it. Optional post-campaign content; the story never
  requires it.

### The Lower Country, by region

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Ilza** | East coast, mid-island. The royal seat and the only real town. Falls before Ch1. |
| **Kureha** | East coast, just south of Ilza. A gate keep on the coast road. |
| **Suza** | The southern cape. The oldest shrine-town on the island and its last free port. |
| **Sarz** | The west valley. The Sarz river rises on Yatan's north face, turns west, and runs seven miles to the sea past Shimba, Kashina and Shena. |
| **Kodo Beach** | Where the Sarz river meets the western sea. Flat sand between cliffs — the one place on this coast an army can land, which is why it always does. |
| **Arn** | West coast, four miles north of Kodo, sheer cliff either side. A river, a natural sea arch, and the oldest shrine in the realm standing over the seal. |
| **Asha Bay** | The great bay in the island's waist — a drowned maze of inlets, headlands and islets with more shoreline than the rest of the coast combined. Impossible to blockade, impossible to navigate without a local. |
| **Kandel** | The ancient border fortress above Asha Bay's south shore. Built four centuries ago to watch the western sea; garrisoned ever since out of habit. |
| **Shirato** | Twin-peaked holy mountain, 518m, above Asha Bay's south shore. Primeval forest, and the northern Sacred Flame. |
| **Yatan** | The island's highest peak, 648m. With Maishi and Taragi it rings the **Uzia basin**, the only wide valley inland. |
| **Taragi** | 558m. Old forest, never cut. Its heart is **the Dreadful Place**, where the 347 are buried and where no layman may set foot. |

**Terrain rules that follow from that shape** and should shape every map:
forest almost everywhere, so movement is slow and cover is cheap; steep
ground with almost no plain; a rias coastline that turns every advance into a
peninsula problem; and cliffs up to a hundred metres on the west, which is why Arn
and Kodo are the only two ways in from that sea.

## The Two Threats

The campaign runs two clocks at different speeds, and they should never feel alike.

1. **The war.** Kurogane is a real army, landed in the west, holding the south and
   the royal seat. Competent commanders following orders they do not enjoy. This is
   what the player fights, chapter to chapter.
2. **The blight.** A line of grey ground creeping inland from the broken seal at
   Arn. It ignores towns. It is walking toward the Flamebrand, and therefore toward
   the party.

Arc 1 is surviving the first. Arc 2 is realising the second exists and is the real
problem. Arcs 3-5 are walking west to end it.

## The Blackflame

Rules the game holds itself to, so the fiction and the mechanics agree:

- **It hollows, it does not burn.** Blighted ground is grey, cold and quiet.
  Nothing rots on it. Nothing eats there.
- **It leaves holes.** An **Abyssal Rift** is where it has fed to completion. No
  heat, no sound, no shadow. Impassable, and it drags at anyone near.
- **It stands its dead back up.** Revenants keep their faces and their kit. This is
  the most upsetting fact in the setting and the writing should treat it that way.
- **It will not cross running water.** Lisette's first proven rule, and the reason the
  Sarz and the Arn river matter tactically.
- **It reaches for the Flamebrand.** The sword is the last thing tied to the seal, so
  the blight tracks it. Carrying it is what makes the party the target — which
  Shigeru works out in Ch5 and can do nothing about.
- **It learns.** From Ch7 it stops behaving like weather and starts behaving like
  something that notices being measured. This is Lisette's crisis.

## The Sacred Flames

Three shrines, three flames, all in the Lower Country:

- **Arn** (west) — over the seal itself. Takeshi broke this one.
- **Shirato** (north) — above Asha Bay. Mirelle's shrine.
- **Suza** (south) — on the cape, the oldest shrine-town.

While the other two burn, the seal is cracked rather than open. The endgame question
is not "kill Takeshi" but: *can the seal be relit, and with what?* The Flamebrand
holds 347 embers. Relighting Arn spends all of them. The sword goes dark and stays
dark.

**The campaign ends where it began**, at Arn — on the cliff above the sea arch,
at the shrine the 347 died to raise.

## Tone Rules

- **No irony about the setting.** Nobody comments on tropes, mechanics, or being in
  a story. Tactical vocabulary (avoid, terrain cost, the weapon triangle) is fine —
  it is how soldiers talk about their trade.
- **Enemies are people with orders.** Almost every boss is doing their job, knows
  something is wrong in the west, and has been told not to look at it. Killing them
  is supposed to cost the player something.
- **Takeshi is not mad.** He is polite, patient, and completely certain. He makes the
  strongest argument in the game for his own position, and Shigeru never wins the
  argument — he only refuses it.
