# Orin — Dancer (The Showman)

- **Class**: Dancer (no weapon)
- **Awareness Tier**: 3 (Genre-Displaced — thinks musical)

## Personality

Dramatic, flamboyant, sincere beneath every flourish. Everything is a performance, but none of it is fake. Speaks in theatrical metaphors — battles are "acts," allies are "the ensemble," death is "the final curtain." His sincerity is what makes the theatrics work instead of being annoying.

## Meta-Angle

Thinks this is a musical. Every battle needs a dance number. Every emotional beat needs a song. Uses Dance to "change the tempo" — and mechanically, he's right. Refresh is the most powerful support action in the game, and Orin treats it with the gravitas of a showstopper.

- "The tempo is wrong! Someone needs to change the rhythm — ah, that's me."
- Calls the enemy phase "the villain's monologue" and the player phase "our number"
- Refers to death as "leaving the stage" and treats it with genuine theatrical grief
- Joins right after Kael's death — frames it as "the tragedy that sets up Act 3"

## What They Learn / What They Don't Know

- Learns that his "performance" framing maps surprisingly well to how the System structures reality — acts, scenes, resets
- Never fully abandons the musical framing but uses it to articulate truths others can't express
- Discovers that Dance doesn't just refresh allies mechanically — it genuinely renews their will to fight
- Doesn't realize his "tempo changes" are subtle manipulations of the System's turn structure

## Relationships

- **Bram**: Both dramatic, instant friendship. Bram is a fighting-game player; Orin is a musical lead. Together they're the loudest, most chaotic duo in the party. Their supports are pure energy.
- **Lira**: Both genre-displaced romantics. Lira sees love stories; Orin sees musicals. They immediately agree the party needs more emotional honesty and less tactical brooding. Co-write an imaginary duet.
- **Ren**: Orin calls Ren "the tragic lead who won't sing his solo." Ren finds him exhausting. But Orin's Dance keeps Ren alive, and Ren grudgingly admits the metaphors sometimes land.
- **Kael** (posthumous): Never met Kael but frames his death as the inciting tragedy. "Every great show needs the moment the audience cries."

## Arc

1. **Arc 2 (Ch9-10)**: Joins in the aftermath of Kael's death. "The ensemble is grieving. They need someone to set the tempo for the next act." Provides levity and mechanical power at the moment both are needed most.
2. **Arc 3 (Ch11-15)**: Corruption threatens to silence the "music." Orin treats corrupted zones as "discordant" and fights to restore harmony. His Dance becomes critical for action economy.
3. **Arc 4 (Ch16-20)**: Realizes his musical framing is closer to truth than anyone thought. The System DOES have a structure, a rhythm, a score. Orin was hearing it all along.
4. **Arc 5 (Ch21-25)**: The final battle is his magnum opus. Dances for every ally who needs one more action. "The finale demands an encore."

## Key Lines

- *"Every battle needs a dance number. I don't make the rules — actually, I do. That's the point."*
- *"You fight. I'll make sure you get to fight again. That's my part in the ensemble."*
- *"The show must go on. Not because it has to — because the cast deserves an ending."*
- *"Curtain call, everyone. Let's make it worth the standing ovation."*
