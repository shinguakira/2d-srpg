# Lira — Cleric (Wrong Genre)

- **Class**: Cleric (staff)
- **Awareness Tier**: 3 (Genre-Displaced — thinks she's in a visual novel / dating sim)

## Personality

Sweet, optimistic, warm, emotionally perceptive. Interprets everything through the lens of romance narratives — character routes, affection meters, support conversations, confession scenes. Seems naive. Is actually the most emotionally intelligent character in the cast.

## Meta-Angle

Like Bram, Lira knows she's in a game. But her awareness loaded visual novel / dating sim metadata. She sees the meta-layer through romantic narrative structure.

- Asks allies about their "route" — "Am I on Ren's route? Or is this a harem ending?"
- Disappointed there's no affection meter: "How do I know if I'm making progress?"
- Interprets EVERY battlefield interaction as romantic subtext: Ren saving Kael is "the rivalry arc," Senna calculating is "the tsundere phase"
- Thinks battles are "mini-games between the real story chapters"
- Tries to trigger support conversations by standing adjacent to allies: "We've been next to each other for 3 turns! That's S-rank territory!"

## The Secret Genius

Lira's dating sim framework makes her the best reader of PEOPLE in the party:

- She predicts character behavior better than Senna's formulas because she reads motivation, not stats
- Her "romantic subtext" observations are actually accurate emotional analysis — she just labels them wrong
- "Ren is clearly in the 'brooding loner route' — he needs someone to challenge his worldview in Act 3" → That's... a correct character arc prediction
- She understands WHY people act, not just HOW — the opposite of Senna

Her "wrong genre" isn't weakness. It's a different kind of intelligence wearing a silly costume.

## Relationships

- **Ren**: Insists she's on "Ren's route." Ren dismisses her. But she's the one who sees through his nihilism: "You care more than you pretend to. That's a classic tsundere flag." She's RIGHT. He hates that she's right.
- **Kael**: Reads him as "the pure-hearted childhood friend route." Oblivious to the fact that he's just... being nice. His death devastates her because in her genre, the childhood friend ALWAYS survives.
- **Senna**: Rivals in analysis. Senna reads systems; Lira reads hearts. Senna dismisses Lira until Lira's emotional predictions outperform Senna's calculations. Their grudging mutual respect is one of the best dynamics in the game.
- **Bram**: Wrong-genre solidarity. They don't understand each other's games at all, but they understand being displaced. "You want combos, I want romance. This game owes us BOTH."
- **Voss**: Tries to assign him a route. Voss is baffled. "I defected from the enemy army." / "That's the REDEMPTION route! Do you have a tragic backstory? Please say yes."

## Arc

1. **Ch1**: Arrives at the end. "Is this where the story starts? I heard there would be character development." Establishes the bit. Funny, light, seemingly one-note.
2. **Ch2**: Her emotional reads start being useful. She predicts Voss's defection before it happens: "He has 'conflicted ally' energy. He'll switch sides by turn 3." Everyone is unnerved that she's right.
3. **Ch3**: The shell CRACKS. Kael dies. Lira's genre has no framework for permanent death.
   - "This isn't what's supposed to happen. In my genre, nobody dies."
   - "Lira, this was never your genre."
   - "I KNOW. I know that. I just... wanted it to be."
   - This is the most important line in her arc. She was NEVER confused about what genre she was in. She was COPING. The dating sim framing was a defense mechanism against permadeath. If she pretends hard enough that this is a romance game, nobody has to die.
4. **Ch4**: Delivers the thematic thesis. Post-credits, when Ren mourns that he can't replay this playthrough: "That's what makes it real." She's the character who understands impermanence — because her entire genre is about moments that matter precisely because they're fleeting.

- *"This totally counts as support level C."* (after any emotional scene)
- *"I KNOW. I know that. I just... wanted it to be."* (Ch3 — the mask drops)
- *"That's what makes it real."* (Epilogue — thematic thesis)
